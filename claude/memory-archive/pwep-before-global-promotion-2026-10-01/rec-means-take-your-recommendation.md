# "rec" means "go with your recommendation"

When I put numbered questions to Sahil with a recommended answer under each, he often accepts
the recommendation rather than writing his own. He has made that a permanent shorthand:

> "go with your recommendation (from now on, i will just say rec when i mean this)"

So an answer of "rec", on its own or against a numbered question, is full acceptance of the
recommendation I gave for that question — not a request to re-explain it, and not partial
agreement. Proceed on it exactly as written. If several questions are answered "rec" in one
message, each one takes its own recommendation.

The obligation this puts on me is to make every recommendation precise enough to be accepted
blind. If a recommendation is vague, "rec" inherits the vagueness and I will have to come
back and ask again, which is the cost he is trying to avoid. So each recommendation should
state the actual choice, the value or name where one is needed, and what it rules out — not
"something along these lines".

He may still answer "rec" and then add guidance in the same message. When he does, the
guidance qualifies the recommendation rather than replacing it; both apply.
