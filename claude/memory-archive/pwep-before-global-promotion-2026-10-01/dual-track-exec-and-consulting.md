# The positioning serves two audiences, not one

Sahil stated this directly while asking for LinkedIn advice on 2026-09-21:

> "i am both trying to get hired as a senior technology executive, AND also
> advertise my consulting on the side"

This is the frame for anything that touches his public positioning — the
`/personal` and `/consulting` sites in `pwep-consulting-website`, his LinkedIn
profile, his resume, and any bio or copy written for him. Do not assume a single
funnel.

The two audiences want different things from the same facts:

- **Executive search** (recruiters, CEOs, boards) reads for title keywords,
  scope, org size, budget, and an unbroken current role. Consulting presented as
  freelancing reads as "between roles"; consulting presented as a practice he
  runs reads as normal and high-status.
- **Consulting buyers** (CTOs, VPs, founders) read for a named problem, a
  concrete deliverable, and evidence the advice has an operating record behind
  it.

The practical consequence: lead with the operating executive identity, because
it is the credential that makes the advisory work believable, and let the
consulting practice be the current chapter rather than a fallback. Where a
surface can be split — LinkedIn's Services section versus the main profile, the
`/consulting` site versus `/personal` — give each audience its own surface
rather than blending both into one compromised message.

When writing anything outward-facing for him, check it against both readers
before handing it over.
