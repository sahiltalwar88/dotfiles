# Change whatever variables you need; realism is the goal

Sahil has said this twice in one session, which makes it a standing position rather than a
one-off. Asked whether I could alter the fir species' proportions, he answered: *"yes, you
can change whatever you like - the goal is to make them look as realistic as possible."*
Later, asked whether to hold some tree settings back: *"feel free to change ANY AND ALL
variables."*

So do not spend a question on permission to change a tuning constant — a species' crown
radius, a height distribution, a density, a mask's construction, a fog curve. Make the
change, put it behind a URL knob with the old value reachable, say what was changed and
why, and let him judge it in the browser. The questions worth asking him are the ones where
different answers lead to genuinely different work, not the ones where he would obviously
say "go ahead".

Two things this does NOT license, and both matter because he has been explicit about them
separately:

- It is not permission to change a look he has already approved without flagging it. He
  distinguishes between exploring parameters, which he wants done freely, and shipping a
  new default, which he wants to decide. When a change would alter the default view — for
  instance cutting leaf-card density tenfold — leave the default alone and hand him the URL.
- It does not lift the standing rule to stop and check in at task boundaries.

The underlying reason is that he judges this project by eye in a browser and cannot tell
from a description whether a parameter change helps. Shipping the change behind a knob and
showing him a rendered frame is always faster than asking.
