# The sky is open for work, and a scoped preference is not a standing rule

An earlier memory recorded that Sahil "significantly prefers the existing sky and
does not want realism passes touching it". **That is wrong as a standing rule.**
When I cited it while planning surface-detail work across the world, he corrected
it directly:

> "that note was not a permanent note, it was for a specific section - i've
> already been improving the sky with other sessions; the aurora for instance has
> been totally redone"

So the sky, the aurora, the clouds and the rest of the celestial layer are
ordinary subjects for improvement like anything else in the world. Do not refuse
or hedge sky work on the strength of the old note, and do not warn him off
touching it.

## The wider lesson, which is the durable part

The original preference was real, but it applied to **one section at one moment**.
I hardened it into a permanent constraint and then carried it forward for weeks,
which cost him a round of being told his own world was off limits. When he
expresses a preference, note the scope he gave it. Words like "for this section",
"for now", "this time" narrow a preference to the thing in front of him; "always",
"never", "whenever you" widen it into a rule. A preference with no explicit scope
attached to a specific piece of work is about that work, not about the whole
project forever.

This matters most for preferences that read as prohibitions, because those are the
ones that silently shrink what I am willing to do for him later. When an old note
would have me tell him "no" about his own project, check whether it was ever meant
that broadly before acting on it — and when in doubt, ask him rather than enforce
it.

## The rule also lives in the repo's documents — strike it there on sight

The same scoped rule was still written into `SUMMIT-PLAN.md` ("The sky is untouched")
and `CONTEXT.md` ("the domain Sahil owns and reserves") weeks later, and I relayed it
to him as a live constraint in a research summary. He corrected it again: "this sky is
able to be changed - please drop that rule wherever you saw it, that was for ONE
session, not a general mandate. feel free to suggest more changes to the sky." So when
a plan or glossary still states a sky restriction, remove it from the document rather
than quoting it, and treat sky changes as welcome proposals.

Even "from now on" can be scoped to the session: when he asked me to leave the
screenshots to him "from now on" because he wanted to get to bed, he meant for that
night only, and corrected me when I saved it as a standing rule. A reason tied to the
moment ("i want to get to bed") marks the instruction as temporary.
