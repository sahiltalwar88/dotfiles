# Offer the cheap route, don't make him arbitrate between routes

When I present Sahil with two implementation routes for the same visual goal — a
safer/cheaper one and a more ambitious one — his answer is reliably some form of
*"go with whichever is easier for now, we can reassess after."* In one round of
six answers on the pwep world he gave that shape of reply three times: on which
of two ways to make the camera skim Saturn's rings ("go with whichever is easier
for now"), on how far to take a grass rewrite ("start with the geometry and just
darken the light 30% for now"), and on what to put in a planet's place ("yes,
just delete for now, comet can come later").

The behavioural change: **stop putting route-choice questions to him.** Take the
cheaper route, state in one line which one I took and what the more ambitious
alternative would buy, and let him ask for the upgrade when he sees the result.
A question that he will answer "whichever is easier" is a question that cost him
a reply and cost me a round trip.

This is not the same as never asking. Questions about *what he wants to see* —
framing, colour, whether a thing should exist at all — are worth asking, because
his answer genuinely changes the target. Questions about *how I get there* are
usually not, because his answer is nearly always "the cheap way, show me, and we
will talk about it then."

The reason is the same reason behind his other standing rules on this project: he
judges the work by eye in a browser, and a cheap version he can look at this
round is worth more to him than an expensive version chosen in the abstract. It
also fits his cost-consciousness — the ambitious route is the one more likely to
be thrown away.
