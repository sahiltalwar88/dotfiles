# When Sahil tells you where a fault is not, that is ground truth

Sahil looks at the `pwep-consulting-website` world in a real browser at full resolution
with the camera moving, which is a strictly better instrument than the headless capture
rig for anything involving motion or sub-pixel detail. When he states where a fault lives
— or, more often, where it does **not** live — that statement is evidence, not a guess to
be verified.

On the river wall flicker he said the artefact was not in the water four separate times
across two sessions, with rising emphasis, ending with:

> "it is NOT IN THE WATER, i cannot stress that enough. it's on the wall behind the water."

He was right every time. Sessions nonetheless kept returning to the water: testing the
reflection terms, the waterline gate, the ripple, the bank reflection. Every one of those
rounds was spent re-checking a region he had already excluded, and the actual fault was on
a vertical wall the whole time.

The rule: when he rules a surface, object or system out, **remove it from the search space
and do not spend a round on it again**. If a diagnostic seems to point back at the thing he
excluded, the diagnostic is more likely mis-designed than he is — check what the diagnostic
actually measures before doubting him. In this case paint modes 1 and 2 both read dark on
the wall not because nothing lit it, but because neither mode included the wall-light term
in what it painted.

The same applies to positive localisation. When he marks a region or names a surface
("the back corner where the river turns left"), take the geometry from that rather than
trying to re-derive it from a capture, and ask for his URL and scroll position if you need
to reproduce the frame.
