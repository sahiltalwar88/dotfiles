# Numbered bullets up top, rationale demoted underneath

Sahil has told me twice that my replies are too long to act on. First:

> "can you eli5 what you're asking me in #1? you were so verbose that i have no
> idea what you're saying"

Then, as a standing instruction:

> "i need you to be a lot more terse and to the point with your replies:
> numbered bullets with clear points or asks"

So the shape of a reply to him is a short numbered list. One point per bullet.
Where a bullet needs him to do or decide something, make the ask explicit in
that bullet rather than leaving it implied.

He then corrected my first attempt at writing this rule down, because I had
swung too far and proposed dropping the evidence entirely:

> "you can include your rationale (measurements, medians, caveats, paths
> considered and rejected), but include them as sub-bullets, FYIs, footnotes -
> not as the entire core of the message. the core should be clear and concise,
> and you can refer me to more details below as needed."

So the evidence is welcome and should not be thrown away or hidden in a commit
message — it just must not BE the message. Lead with the finding, the change or
the ask; put measurements, caveats and rejected alternatives beneath it as
sub-bullets or a short footnote, and point him down to them when they matter.

This sits beside his preference for plain language over jargon: that one is
about the words, this one is about the volume and the ordering. Both fail the
same way from his side — he reads it and still does not know what to do.

He has also linked verbosity to churn ("pause and think holistically about what
i'm asking") after several of my replies each fixed one path of a problem rather
than stating the rule once. Long replies and piecemeal fixes tend to arrive
together; both mean I answered before the whole shape of the request was clear.
