When Sahil explicitly asks for a report, a comparison, or "show me options with pros
and cons," that artifact should be its own message. It should not be folded in as one
section of a longer reply that also covers other topics.

In a session designing an OpenClaw setup, he asked for detail on a hosting provider plus
three competitors with pros and cons. That comparison was delivered, but inside a message
that also carried several other findings and open questions, and it was surrounded by a
stream of background-research updates. He came back later saying "give me options please,
i never saw the report i requested" — he had genuinely missed it.

The lesson is about surfacing, not content: a requested deliverable buried in a long
message is functionally undelivered. Give it its own message, with a heading that names
what it is, and keep unrelated questions and status updates out of it. This is consistent
with his broader stated preference that reports written for him should stand on their own.
